*Please put on your seatbelts — you will ride through a roller coaster of my emotions as I attempt to complete the Tata Mumbai Marathon. The 42.2-kilometer distance brings with it a range of thoughts as we run through the streets, passing landmarks, seeing the scenery change, handling our pains, and celebrating our finish. I hope you enjoy the journey.*

---

## Prologue

A light sleeper at best, Mumbai opened its eyes by 3 a.m. to the trundling arrival of runners on the streets making their way to Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj Terminus (CST), the start point for the marathon. Local trains started to ply around the same time, carrying cheery runners from the suburbs through the arteries of the metropolis. The mismatched rhythm of footsteps, words spoken in half and shouted in half a dozen languages, police announcements, motor horns — all blended into the gargling score of the city street.

---

*"It's finally happening."*

I have waited for this day for 364 days. Just a day after I completed my last Mumbai Marathon in 2022, I knew I must hire myself a coach for better timing this year. Gautam Pothineni, my coach, or *PondiExpress*, is not the fastest runner, but he has committed several mistakes in his running life. I was eager to learn from his experiences of "what not to do" rather than "what to do". One such mistake was not consuming enough carbohydrates before the run. So, I carefully packed two peanut butter sandwiches and a banana at 3:30 a.m. for my one-hour train ride to CST.

Soon, I was at the start line in category 'A', and the realization sank in — it's finally happening. I squeezed a chocolate flavored energy gel; this was one of five gels that I would consume. Everything was planned; we had a strategy in place. After training for approximately 1,900 kms and 200 hours, it's showtime.

---

*"Wow, that is easy. I'm killing it."*

I glided through the first 9 kilometers with ease, navigating through the slower runners. My strategy was to focus on the shoes of a runner a few strides ahead of me, overtake, and repeat — maintaining my marathon pace throughout.

You pass the historic landmarks in this segment — *Flora Fountain* and *Oval Maidan* — before taking a left from *Pizza By The Bay* to enter Marine Drive. This flat road is a treat for runners, but you should be cautious not to throttle and reserve your energy for the hills that will come in the second segment.

---

*"It's amazing how data-driven today's marathons are."*

One more thing Coach Pondi did was introduce science to my training regimen. We used to monitor pace zones, heart rates, lactate threshold, VO2 max, and other metrics. These sensors have become intelligent enough to guesstimate your marathon completion times.

---

*"Mumbai puts out its heart for this day."*

As you continue your miles from *Trident Hotel* to *Babulnath Temple*, you find residents come out and cheer you on. There are NGOs doing Bhaangra and Lavani, the Western Naval Command band plays old Bollywood relics. As you take a sharp right to exit the *Chowpatty* signal, you have a senior citizens' group playing the harmonica. It is the spirit of Mumbai and its beautiful culture that bring people from far away to do this marathon.

---

*"I want to have Penne Arrabbiata."*

It's ascent time. As you pass through the *Peddar Road* flyover — a 20-meter elevation — you don't want to think about running. Only happy thoughts. Penne Arrabbiata has been my soul food for a long time now, and it is the meal I will have after completing this marathon. A pasta dish becomes my prime motivation as I drop my pace slightly and hydrate.

---

*"Trust the process, Viraj."*

Not many may realize, but there is a gradual elevation as you enter the Bandra-Worli Sea Link and exit the *Worli Sea Face*. With the sun rising behind us and crows cawing, your body starts to show slight discomfort, and humidity increases as you are in the middle of the sea. It is for this segment that we had those long runs every Sunday — it was boring, but it was important. As I squeeze the vanilla-flavored energy gel, I tell myself to trust the process.

---

*"Cadbury Fuse…who gives Cadbury Fuse?"*

Businesses are beautiful; strategy makes them sexy — that's why I did an MBA. Who would have thought that a chocolate company could position itself as a "nutrition partner" for a physical endurance event? A volunteer handed me a bar, and that's when I found my new Penne Arrabbiata — my new love for pre-run, during-run, and post-dinner snacking. As I exited the Sea Link to enter *Promenade*, I smiled for the first time in 22 kilometers, and it was because of Cadbury Fuse.

---

*"Left calf…calm down. Brain, don't focus on left calf, please."*

The happiness from Cadbury Fuse was short-lived. My left calf began to twitch, and I feared a further strain would result in a full cramp. This is what is interesting about long-distance running: the journey involves so many dynamic variables, and you must solve these challenges to progress. I decided to gently press my right hand on my right abdomen to divert my attention away from my left calf.

---

*"Why would I pay money to be in this situation?"*

Many say that the actual marathon starts after 32 kilometers. There are no easy points in the last 10 kilometers. How you feel can be very different from what your watch reflects. It can be brutal, and you can hit the wall at any time.

---

*"Be brave, Viraj…The Peddar Road hill is coming."*

The exhaustion has set in. By embracing bravery, you remind yourself that each step in the 25-meter elevation is a courageous act. The running community has a nickname for this hill: "heartbreak hill". For pro-runners, this is the logical section to make an attack if they are feeling strong; it leads to heartbreak for those who can't sustain the speeds.

---

*"Somehow, there are only 6 km to go, which is only 2 km from there being only 2 km to go before there is 2 km to go!"*

It's funny how you fall back on elementary math to break down the milestone into parts so that you can console your mind better, as it's facing its hardest test. We do this routinely without realizing — with time when in the examination hall, with distance when in Bengaluru traffic, and with money when looking at a fine-dining menu.

---

*"Marine Drive…we are friends, right?"*

The route from *Chowpatty* to the finish line is flat but not easy. The sun gives you all its rays, and you are fighting humidity. Towards my right are some new apartments — *Eiffel Enclave* and *Belle View* — that are springing up. It's funny; you could put all the Italian or French you wanted in their building names, but you couldn't take Mumbai out of the buildings. The clothes drying outside the windows would remain, as would the mud streaks from flowerpots on windowsills.

---

*"I am actually close now."*

As you take a sharp left to say goodbye to the sea view, you notice that the roads are now wider, and Mumbaikars take advantage of this to come near you and shout, *"This is the final stretch"*, *"Bas Hogaya, Come On..."* It truly tells a lot about the people of Mumbai — their instinct to support and encourage. It reflects the values of leading with heart.

---

*"42 kilometers are done. Can I stop running, please?"*

At this point, I am just a spectator. It's my legs and mind shouting at each other. With the finish line in sight, I was flying. My shoes suddenly got springs, which seemed to have been absent all this while. That's when you validate that this sport is more mental than physical. The cheering gets louder and louder with each meter.

---

*"Viraj, remember to smile for the finish line photos."*

People forget to smile. It's not just for the cameras; it is also to show gratitude to your legs, arms, mind, and muscles for backing you through the tough ordeal you put them through.

I am well within my targeted marathon timing as I check my watch to end my run. *"Marathon Personal Record"*, my watch flashes. As I open my mouth for a strong exhale, I can taste salt.

Are those tears or sweat? I ask myself.

---

*"This was the worst experience of my life."*

Trust me, you get this thought for a split second — why did you do what you did? All the types of hormones you learned in Class IX are running high. You purposely bend your knee to submit yourself to the earth, but a volunteer hands you a towel and asks you to keep moving forward.

---

*"This was the best experience of my life."*

As you start walking to collect your medal, you realize it's about human grit, about venturing beyond one's comfort zone, and about overcoming challenges. More than the physical, you develop the mental resilience and endurance to go through multiple stages of life. Everyone who reached the finish line learned something new about themselves.

Runners leave the *Azad Maidan* with newfound hopes, much like the hundreds of budding cricketers who play on this ground every day. The beauty is, everyone who leaves this ground is more confident about their future — much to its name.

---

*"Wait…Cramps Cramps Cramps!"*

Well, that cramp story is for another day.
